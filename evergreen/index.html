keelapps/Evergreen

For teams whose knowledge base is quietly rotting

A page that was right two years ago still looks right today.

Knowledge bases rot silently — outdated runbooks look exactly as authoritative as current ones, and nobody can tell the difference from the outside. Evergreen gives every Confluence page a freshness status, and gives owners a one-click way to say “I checked, it's still correct” — without editing the page, creating a version, or pinging a single watcher.

Coming to the Atlassian Marketplace No egress. Runs entirely on Forge — nothing leaves your Atlassian tenant.

The tools in this category are full content-lifecycle suites: archiving workflows, approval chains, automated clean-up jobs. They have proved teams will pay to keep a knowledge base from rotting — and they are a lot of machinery when all that was wanted is a red/yellow/green signal and a button that says it's still fine. Evergreen deliberately stops there.

What it does

A badge on every page

Fresh, Aging or Stale — right in the byline, based on when the page was last edited and when someone last vouched for it. Readers see at a glance whether to trust what they're reading.

Confirm in one click

“Mark as fresh” records who checked and when. It does not edit the page, does not create a new version, and does not notify watchers — the confirmation lives beside the page, not in it.

A dashboard per space

How many pages are fresh, aging and stale; the pages needing attention sorted worst-first; filters by status and owner; and bulk actions for confirming or excluding a whole section at once.

Thresholds that layer

Site defaults (90 / 180 days), overridable per space by space admins, and per page from the byline. The most specific layer wins; everything else inherits.

Exclusions that make sense

A label (evergreen-exempt by default), a whole space, or a single page from its byline. Templates are never tracked at all.

It will not touch your pages

No archiving, no moving, no deleting, no approval workflow, no email. The biggest objection to lifecycle tools — “it will quietly bury something we need” — cannot happen here.

From install to a trustworthy space, end to end

  1. Install and accept the defaults

    Aging after 90 days, stale after 180. The nightly scan indexes every space it can see; there is nothing to model and nobody to train.

  2. Open the space dashboard

    Space settings → Evergreen. The counts say how bad it is; the “Needs attention” list says exactly where to start, worst first.

  3. Work the list

    Pages that are still right get confirmed — one click each, or two hundred at a time with a bulk action. Pages that shouldn't be tracked get excluded. Pages someone else must judge go on the review list.

  4. Let the badge do the talking

    From then on, every reader sees the status on the page itself, and every confirmation resets the clock. The dashboard stays quiet until something actually goes stale.
