keelapps/Signoff

For teams whose pages need a real sign-off

Who approved this version of this page — and is that still good?

Confluence cannot answer that question. Signoff can, because its approvals pin the exact page version they covered: edit the page and the badge degrades to “Approved · page changed” on its own, computed live against the page and never stored. Policies are defined once and bound to spaces by reference — govern three hundred spaces without copying a workflow into any of them.

Coming to the Atlassian Marketplace Zero egress. Approval records never leave your Atlassian tenant.

Quality manuals, SOPs, release notes, legal copy — pages whose whole value is that someone stood behind a specific version of them. The native page has no approval concept at all, and the apps that add one are heavyweight workflow engines configured space by space by hand, with records held on servers outside Atlassian.

What it does

Sign-off in one click

Request approval from the page itself: approvers come from the space's policy or your own pick, any-one or all-must-approve, with an optional note to the approvers.

A badge that cannot lie

An approval names the page version it covered. The badge compares that against the live page every time it renders — “Approved · v12” becomes “page changed” the moment anyone edits, with re-approval one click away.

One policy, every space

Define approvers, mode and validity once. Spaces bind by reference or simply inherit the site default; editing the policy updates every bound space at once. Nothing is ever copied.

Approvals that expire

A policy can give approvals a validity of 3 to 24 months. A daily scan surfaces everything due for re-approval — site-wide for admins, and in each signer's own inbox.

Notified by Confluence itself

Approvers are @-mentioned in a page comment, so requests reach them through Confluence's own bell and email — and the approval trail is visible on the page. Admins can switch the comments off.

An audit trail an auditor can use

Every request, decision and cancellation, exportable as CSV: who approved which version of which page, and when. Built on Forge, zero external calls.

An SOP review, end to end

  1. The policy exists already

    The site admin defined “Quality documents” once: two named approvers, everyone must approve, valid for twelve months. The QMS space inherits it without any setup.

  2. The author requests sign-off

    One click on the page. The approvers are @-mentioned in a comment and the request lands in their approvals inbox; the badge reads “In review”.

  3. Approvers decide from the queue

    Approve or request changes right from the inbox, with a comment. The moment the request settles, the badge reads “Approved · v7, valid until next August”.

  4. The truth maintains itself

    Someone edits the page in March — the badge degrades to “page changed” by itself. Next August the approval expires and the page appears on the due list. Nobody had to remember anything.
