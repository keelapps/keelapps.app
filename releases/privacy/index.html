Privacy policy — Releases for Bitbucket

Effective date: 22 November 2026

The short version

Releases for Bitbucket (“the app”) is published by Keelapps. It is a Forge app that reads a repository's tags, commits, pull requests, build results and Downloads, and shows them as releases inside Bitbucket. It sends nothing anywhere outside Bitbucket. The app stores nothing and declares no external network permissions, which Atlassian enforces at the platform level. There is no Keelapps server, no analytics, no telemetry and no third party.

What the app reads

When someone opens the Releases page, a release, or a repository's Source page, the app reads, as that person and with their Bitbucket permissions:

  • the repository's tags, with their messages, dates and taggers;
  • the commits between a release and the one before it, with their authors and messages;
  • the changelog file at the tagged commit, when there is one;
  • the merged pull requests behind those commits, with their authors, participants and approvals;
  • the build statuses and Code Insights reports on the tagged commit;
  • the names, sizes and dates of the files in the repository's Downloads.

What it reads is shown on the page and then discarded. The app has no storage of any kind. It reads one changelog file at a tag and never reads the rest of your source code.

What it never does

  • No data leaves Bitbucket. The app cannot make an outbound call. There is no analytics, no telemetry and no third-party service.
  • Nothing is written. It requests read scopes only: no write to repositories, tags, pull requests or Downloads, and no storage.
  • Exports stay with you. The Markdown and JSON exports are built in the page and copied by the person viewing it; Keelapps never receives them.
  • The vendor cannot browse your data. What Keelapps can see is the app's logs: like every Forge app's, they are shared with the developer by default, and your workspace administrator can switch that off. They hold the error text Bitbucket returns when a request fails, which can name a repository, a tag or a commit, and never release notes, commit messages or code. Support requests are answered from what you tell us, not from your data.
  • The app requests no scope beyond the two listed in its documentation.

Retention and deletion

The app keeps nothing, so there is nothing for it to delete. Uninstalling it removes it from your workspace and leaves your repositories exactly as they were.

Data residency

Whatever your Bitbucket Cloud workspace's is. The app stores nothing, so Releases for Bitbucket introduces no additional residency considerations.

Sub-processors

None. Atlassian hosts the app.

Changes

Material changes to what is read, written or where will be reflected here and in the Marketplace listing before they ship.

Contact

support@keelapps.atlassian.net — for questions about this policy, and for data access, correction or deletion requests.
